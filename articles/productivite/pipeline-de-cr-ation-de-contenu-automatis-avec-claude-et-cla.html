how I automate my X contents pipeline using claude code.
most creators are doing this the slow way. they're manually searching for trending topics, writing threads from scratch, designing banners in canva, creating infographics in excalidraw and cleaning up and editing videos.
I used to do the same thing. now I don't.
it doesn't really matter which model you're working with, I use claude personally, but this same setup works with gemini or any other LLM model.
here's the exact system I built to automate my content pipeline: — scraping hot and trending topics on X, — generating articles body in my voice (I manually edit it before posting, I'm from the "never let AI post for you" school of thought), — generating banners and excalidraw infographic, — and editing video clips
all without doing 80% of the work manually, think for a moment how much time that would save you if you have a system like it.

#1 — Setting Up the CLAUDE.md (the architecture)

before i show you the tools, you need to understand the structure. because without this, you're just asking an AI to do things and hoping it's consistent.
the whole system is set up through a single file: CLAUDE.md. it's the first thing claude reads when it opens the project it's a set of instructions for how everything works:
— it defines the three-layer architecture (directives, orchestration, execution) — it tells claude where to look for existing scripts before it tries to write new ones — it lays out a self-annealing loop, when something breaks, claude reads the error, fixes the script, and updates the directive so the same failure doesn't happen twice.
here's why this matters:
LLMs are probabilistic in nature, not deterministic. if you ask an AI the same question twice, you won't always get the same answer.
it's making educated guesses every single time, not running a fixed process. that's fine for brainstorming, but it's a problem when you're building a content pipeline that needs to work the same way every time you run it.
CLAUDE.md fixes this by separating what the AI is good at from what code is good at. python scripts don't guess, they run the same logic every time.
so instead of asking claude to do everything itself, it reads the situation, decides which script to call, and checks the output. the heavy lifting is deterministic. the decision-making is where the AI earns its place.

google docs
, simply copy and tell claude or any model you're using to setup your workspace with CLAUDE.md, GEMINI.md etc.

#2 — Scraping X for Content Ideas

finding what to write about is probably where most creators waste the most time. scrolling X for inspiration, saving posts to notes, trying to reverse-engineer what's actually performing in your niche. you can automate all of this.
you can make claude do the work for you using the X API, it feels illegal but don't stress, it's actually completely legal for you to use.
I gave claude a list of accounts i personally enjoy and learn from in my niche, told it what kind of content performs well for my audience, and asked it to build a scraper that pulls trending posts, ranks them by engagement, and surfaces the top ideas.
the whole thing runs in under 2 minutes.
I gave it a list of accounts I personally enjoy and learn from in my niche, told it what kind of content performs well for my audience, and asked it to build a scraper that pulls trending posts, ranks them by engagement, and surfaces the top ideas. the whole thing runs in under 2 minutes.
here's roughly how i prompted claude to build the system for me:
"I want to scrape X for trending content in my niche. my niche is AI tools, agentic AI, and crypto opportunities. pull the top performing posts from accounts i follow and from keyword searches across these topics. rank results by engagement — likes, retweets, replies. then pass the top 30 posts to you and generate 5 content topic ideas tailored to my account style and audience. save the output so i can review and pick the best one."
that's it. claude built the python scripts, wired up the API, and set up the ranking logic. btw, I don't understand the python codes nor do i review it for accuracy, claude does all of that for me, I only review the output, make a few adjustments, and it's been running the same way ever since.
now every time I want fresh topic ideas, I run one command and get 5 data-backed ideas in under 2 minutes.

#3 — Writing Article

this is where i actually do some work — emphasis on some.
once i've selected a topic, I tell claude to generate the article structure first. like the example below, it maps the skeleton before writing a single word.
from there, I have a separate execution script that goes out and does the research for me. it scrapes X and youtube for relevant content on the topic, finds the top performing posts and videos, then pulls the transcripts using
apify
(a third party app for scrapping social media).
so claude isn't just working off a hunch, it's working off actual data from both platforms before it writes anything. because claude already understands my style and tone, the output it generates is very close to how I would have written it myself.
but i do still make edits, add humor, cut things that feel too stiff, rephrase lines that don't sound like me. it's less writing and more editing, which is a very different workload.
the best part is that every correction i make teaches the system. every edit, every adjustment, every time i say "no, not like that" — claude takes it into context and gets better at writing like me. the more i use it, the less i have to fix.
as I've said earlier i'm from the "never let AI post for you" school of thought. the AI builds the foundation, I make it human.

#5 — Creating Article Banners & Infographics

before i started using claude, my graphic workflow looked like this: open canva for banners, switch to excalidraw for infographics, spend 30 minutes adjusting layouts, second-guess the design, and end up with something that looked just okay. every single post.
that changed completely when i built my own infographic agentic skills inside claude. now every banner and infographic I use is generated by claude directly. no canva, no excalidraw, no switching between tools.
here's what makes it even better.
whenever i come across an infographic i like on my timeline — a layout that catches my eye, a structure that communicates something really well — i copy the image, send it to claude, and tell it to use that as a reference.
claude studies the design, understands the structure, and applies it to my content. i'm not starting from scratch anymore, i'm upgrading what already works.
and the actual prompt I use is almost embarrassingly simple:
"hey, i need a comprehensive infographic for this drafted post, use the infographic skill to create a perfect befitting graphic in X portrait image standard."
then I paste the content of my draft and press enter.
in less than 5 minutes i have a graphic that's ready to use. no back and forth, no resizing, no font hunting. just a finished asset sitting in my folder waiting to be posted.
the difference in output quality compared to what I was manually producing in canva is honestly not even close.

#6 — How It All Fits Together

here's what the actual workflow looks like end to end.
  • Step 1: run the content strategy script, it scrapes trending posts on X, ranks them by engagement, and returns 5 data-backed topic ideas in under 2 minutes.
  • Step 2: pick the strongest topic, review the ideas, and decide which one fits your audience right now.
  • Step 3: run the youtube scraper, it finds relevant content on the topic, pulls transcripts, and hands everything to claude to generate a full article in your voice.
  • Step 4: open the draft, make your edits, add humor, cut what doesn't sound like you, adjust the tone. this is the part you actually do yourself.
  • Step 5: prompt claude with the infographic skill — paste your draft, press enter, and in under 5 minutes you have a banner and infographic ready to use.
what's still manual: picking the right topic, editing the article, and the final decision to post. everything else is automated.
the honest version of this workflow is that i spend most of my time editing and refining, not creating from scratch.
and every correction i make trains the system to need less correction next time. that's the compounding effect nobody talks about when they say "use AI for content." it doesn't get worse over time. it gets better.
