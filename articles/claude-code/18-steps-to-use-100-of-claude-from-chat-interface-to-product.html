Foundation Steps: The Context Layer

Step 1: Write a CLAUDE.md Before Every Project
The single most impactful thing you can do to improve Claude's outputs costs you 30 minutes and pays back in every session forever.
A CLAUDE.md is a persistent context file that Claude reads before every session in a Claude Code project.
Without it: Claude starts every session knowing nothing about your project, your standards, or your preferences.
With it: Claude starts every session knowing everything a senior team member who has worked with you for months would know.
# Project CLAUDE.md ## What We Are Building [One clear sentence] ## Tech Stack [Exact versions and choices] ## Architecture Decisions [Key decisions already made that Claude should not reverse] ## Standards [Code style, naming conventions, test requirements] ## Current Focus [What we are working on right now] ## Hard Rules [What Claude must never do without explicit confirmation]
Fill every section with specifics. Vague inputs produce vague outputs. Specific inputs produce a Claude that operates like it has been on your project for months.
Step 2: Build a Voice Profile for Every Writing Task
Generic Claude content sounds like generic AI content.
A precisely configured voice profile produces content that sounds like you wrote it at your best.
Before writing anything important, run this prompt first:
Analyze these [10] samples of my writing and extract: 1. Average sentence length and variation patterns 2. Capitalization habits and strategic emphasis 3. How I open paragraphs versus how I close them 4. Vocabulary level and specific word choices I favor 5. What I never say and phrases I consistently avoid 6. How I handle transitions between ideas 7. My signature closing moves [PASTE YOUR 10 BEST WRITING SAMPLES]
Save the extracted profile. Add it to your CLAUDE.md for content projects. Every piece Claude writes from that file will sound like you.
Step 3: Use Claude Projects for Persistent Context
Claude Projects maintain context across sessions.
Everything you upload to a Project as knowledge lives in that Project permanently. Every conversation in the Project has access to that knowledge.
The difference between a session and a Project:
Session: Claude knows what you tell it today and nothing else.
Project: Claude knows everything you have ever uploaded to the Project permanently.
For any work that continues across multiple days create a Project. Upload your CLAUDE.md, your reference documents, your previous outputs, and your relevant research.
Every conversation in that Project starts from complete context.
Step 4: Front-load Context Before Every Complex Request
Most people write the request and then add context as afterthoughts.
Invert this.
Context first. Request second.
CONTEXT: - I am building [specific thing] - The primary constraint is [specific constraint] - The audience is [specific description] - What I have already tried: [what failed and why] - The decision that cannot change: [locked decisions] REQUEST: [What you actually want] OUTPUT FORMAT: [Exactly what you want back]
Structured context produces structured outputs. Structured outputs require less editing.
Step 5: Define the Output Format Explicitly
Claude produces better outputs when it knows exactly what format you want before it starts.
Never leave format to inference.
For every significant request add an output specification:
Produce this as: - [FORMAT TYPE] - [APPROXIMATE LENGTH] - [SPECIFIC SECTIONS REQUIRED] - [WHAT TO INCLUDE] - [WHAT TO EXCLUDE] - [TONE]
The more specific the format specification the closer the first output will be to what you actually want.

Prompting Steps: The Quality Layer

Step 6: Use the Persona Assignment Technique
Claude reasons differently when assigned a specific perspective.
Generic prompt: "Review my business plan."
Persona prompt: "You are a Series B investor who has seen 2,000 pitches. Your job is not to be encouraging. Find every reason this fails. Be specific about the three assumptions the entire plan rests on that might be wrong."
The persona changes the frame of analysis. The frame of analysis changes the depth of output.
Assign specific personas for specific tasks:
  • Code review: "You are a senior engineer focused on production failure modes"
  • Strategy: "You are a CEO who has run this exact business for 10 years"
  • Writing: "You are an editor at [specific publication] with [specific standards]"
Step 7: Request the Pre-Mortem Before Every Major Output
Before Claude produces any significant output ask it to pre-mortem its own response.
Before you answer, tell me: 1. The three ways your answer might be wrong 2. The assumption you are making that I have not confirmed 3. What additional information would change your answer significantly 4. The scenario where your answer is exactly wrong Then give me your answer.
This forces Claude to surface uncertainty explicitly rather than expressing it through hedged language you have to read carefully.
Step 8: Use Negative Examples as Much as Positive Ones
Most people show Claude examples of what they want.
Showing Claude examples of what they do not want is equally powerful.
For any output with a quality standard add a negative example:
Here is an example of the output I want: [GOOD EXAMPLE] Here is an example of what I do not want: [BAD EXAMPLE] Specifically what makes the bad example wrong: - [SPECIFIC ISSUE 1] - [SPECIFIC ISSUE 2] - [SPECIFIC ISSUE 3] Now produce the output I need: [REQUEST]
Negative constraints eliminate the most common failure modes without requiring Claude to infer what you dislike.
Step 9: Chain Prompts for Complex Tasks
Complex tasks produce better outputs when broken into a chain rather than requested in one shot.
One-shot: "Write me a complete marketing strategy."
Chained:
Step 1: "Analyze the target customer. Tell me what they want, what they fear, and what they believe."
Step 2: "Based on your customer analysis, identify the three strongest positioning angles."
Step 3: "Develop the positioning angle you ranked highest into a complete messaging framework."
Step 4: "Turn the messaging framework into a channel strategy."
Each step in the chain builds on the previous one with full context. The final output is significantly better than any one-shot attempt could produce.
Step 10: Ask Claude to Steelman the Opposite View
For any decision, strategy, or plan ask Claude to make the strongest possible case against your position before recommending it.
I am planning to [DECISION]. Before you help me execute this, make the strongest possible argument against it. Not a weak counterargument. The argument that would make a smart person choose the opposite. Then tell me how you would respond to that argument if you were defending my position. Then give me your honest recommendation.
The steelman process forces Claude to engage with genuine objections rather than confirming your existing view.

Configuration Steps: The System Layer

Step 11: Build Skill Files for Every Repeating Task
A skill file is a reusable workflow you write once and call forever.
Every time you write the same complex prompt from scratch you are wasting time and producing inconsistent outputs.
The skill file format:
# [SKILL NAME] ## Purpose [What this skill does in one sentence] ## Trigger How to invoke: "Run [skill name] on [input]" ## Context Required [What Claude should read before running this skill] ## Process [Step-by-step what Claude does] ## Output Format [Exactly what the output looks like] ## Quality Standard [What a great output looks like vs an acceptable one] ## Edge Cases [How to handle inputs that do not fit the standard pattern]
Build one skill file per week for your five most repeated tasks. Within five weeks you have a library that eliminates the most expensive prompt-writing work in your operation.
Step 12: Configure MCP Servers for Your Tool Stack
Most Claude users are operating with Claude disconnected from every tool they actually use.
MCP servers connect Claude to your real environment.
The highest-value connections for most operators:
Filesystem MCP: Claude reads and writes your local files directly.
GitHub MCP: Claude operates in your actual codebase.
Notion MCP: Claude reads and updates your project management.
Supabase MCP: Claude queries your actual database in plain English.
Slack MCP: Claude reads your team communications and sends updates.
Each connection eliminates a category of manual context-passing. Instead of pasting your database schema into every session Claude reads the actual schema. Instead of describing your project status Claude reads your actual Notion database.
Step 13: Use Claude Code for Autonomous Multi-Step Work
Claude through the web interface requires you in the loop at every step.
Claude Code runs autonomously across multiple steps without you in the loop.
The /goal command is what makes autonomous work reliable.
You set the goal at the start of the session. Claude checks every action against the goal before taking it. It continues until the goal is reached without drifting into sub-problems.
For any task with more than three sequential steps use Claude Code with /goal rather than the web interface.
The quality of autonomous multi-step work in Claude Code compared to the web interface is not a subtle difference.
Step 14: Build a System Prompt Library
Most people write system prompts from scratch for each new use case.
Building a library of tested system prompts that you refine over time compounds your output quality automatically.
The system prompt categories worth building:
Research analyst: Deep research with source citation standards and uncertainty flagging.
Code reviewer: Production-focused review with specific failure mode detection.
Strategic advisor: Devil's advocate analysis with explicit assumption surfacing.
Content editor: Voice-matched editing with specific quality standards.
Business analyst: Framework-driven analysis with explicit recommendation format.
Each system prompt in your library represents hours of iteration compressed into a reusable asset.

Advanced Steps: The Intelligence Layer

Step 15: Upload Reference Material as Permanent Context
Claude in a Project with your reference material uploaded reasons from that material rather than from general training data.
The difference is the difference between an analyst who has read your research and one who has not.
Material worth uploading to persistent Projects:
Your own best work: Previous outputs that Claude can pattern-match against.
Domain references: The books, papers, and guides that define your field's standards.
Business context: Your company's history, positioning, and strategic decisions.
Personal frameworks: The mental models and decision frameworks you use consistently.
Each upload compounds the intelligence of every subsequent conversation in that Project.
Step 16: Use Extended Thinking for Decisions That Matter
For complex decisions Claude's extended thinking mode produces qualitatively different analysis.
Extended thinking gives Claude more space to reason through a problem before committing to an answer.
Use it for:
  • Architectural decisions with long-term implications
  • Strategic choices between genuinely different paths
  • Analysis where the right answer is not obvious
  • Any decision where you want to see the reasoning not just the conclusion
The prompt that unlocks extended thinking:
This decision matters and I want you to think it through carefully before answering. Work through the problem step by step. Show your reasoning at each step. Identify where you are uncertain. Consider the implications you might normally skip. [YOUR QUESTION]
Step 17: Build a Feedback Loop Into Every Session
Most people use Claude in one direction. They request. Claude produces. They accept or reject.
Building a feedback loop makes every session improve the next one.
At the end of every significant Claude session run this:
Review the outputs you produced in this session. Tell me: 1. Where your outputs were strongest and why 2. Where your outputs fell short of what I needed 3. What additional context from me would have improved your outputs 4. What patterns you noticed in my requests that suggest I should structure my prompts differently Save this as a session reflection note.
Over 30 sessions the feedback loop reveals patterns in how you prompt that you could not see from inside any individual session.
Step 18: Treat Configuration as a Compounding Asset
The final step is not a technique. It is a mindset.
Every CLAUDE.md you write is an asset that pays back in every session that uses it.
Every skill file you build is an asset that eliminates work every time you call it.
Every system prompt you refine is an asset that improves every output it generates.
Every piece of reference material you upload is an asset that deepens analysis in every conversation that references it.
Most people treat every Claude session as an isolated interaction.
The people getting exceptional results treat their Claude configuration as a compounding asset portfolio.
The CLAUDE.md gets better every time you update it.
The skill files get more precise every time you run them and observe the edge cases.
The system prompts get more calibrated every time you compare outputs across different versions.
The reference material gets richer every time you add a document that performed well in a session.
None of this requires exceptional prompting skill.
It requires the discipline to save the work you do in each session as an asset for the next one.
