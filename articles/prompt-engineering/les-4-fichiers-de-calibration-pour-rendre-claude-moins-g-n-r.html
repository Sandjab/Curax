Why Claude Is Dumb By Default

Out of the box, Claude knows nothing about you.
It has the training data of the internet and a system prompt from Anthropic. That's it.
Every output is the average of every Claude user's output for that prompt. Average is generic. Generic doesn't ship.
The fix is feeding Claude 4 files of your context at the start of every session. Each file does one specific job.

File 1. CLAUDE. md

The orchestrator.
Sits at the root of your folder. Tells Claude where the other 3 files live.
Plus the 5-10 hard rules you'd repeat in every conversation otherwise.
Under 80 lines. The shorter the better.

File 2. SOUL. md

Who you are.
Your values, your way of working, your taste. The things that make you a specific person and not a generic AI user.
Claude reads this so every output sounds like you. Specific examples beat adjectives every time.
50 lines max.

File 3. VOICE. md

The writing rules.
Sentence patterns, banned words, tone, plus 10-20 voice samples from your best writing.
This is the file that closes the gap from "sounds like AI" to "sounds like the writer." It compounds the fastest of the four.
Update monthly as your style evolves.

File 4. CONSTRAINTS. md

The always-nos.
Things Claude must never do. Formats to avoid. Phrases that get cut on sight.
Your taste, encoded. Once written, you stop fighting the same fights every output.
Add a line every time you reject an output. The file gets sharper week by week.

How To Drop Them In

Three steps.
Step 1. Create a folder anywhere on your computer. Drop the 4 files into it.
Step 2. In CLAUDE. md, write 5 lines telling Claude where the other 3 files live and the rules to load at session start.
Step 3. Tell Claude to read CLAUDE. md at the start of every session. Done.
The first chat after setup, you'll feel the difference. By session three, the rewrite step disappears entirely.

What Outputs Look Like After

That gap shows up across every piece of work you produce.
